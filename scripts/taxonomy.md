# Labelling guide

You are labelling passages of scripture by **what the passage is doing** — its
form and rhetorical force — not by its subject matter and not by whether you
agree with it. This is ordinary literary form-criticism, the same sorting a
scholar of any of these traditions would recognize.

Seven codes. Assign a code only when that force is clearly present in the
passage itself, not implied by context you are inferring.

**How many codes.** As many as are genuinely there, and no more. One is the
common case and two is frequent; three is rare, and a fourth is never allowed.
Do not pad a passage out to a second code, and do not squeeze a passage that is
plainly doing two things down to one. A long passage does not earn more codes
than a short one.

| Code | Name | Assign when the passage… |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `L` | Law | instructs, commands, prohibits, or prescribes. Imperative force aimed at the hearer: statutes, ritual rules, monastic precepts, "thou shalt", "you should", "let him". |
| `P` | Promise | holds out a good outcome: blessing, paradise, prosperity, forgiveness, merit, liberation, reward. Conditional or unconditional. |
| `T` | Threat | holds a bad outcome **over the hearer**: curse, punishment, hell, woe, the consequence of disobedience. Somebody is being warned. |
| `V` | Violence | describes harm done: killing, war, slaughter, torture, execution, destruction. What happened or is happening, whoever it happens to. |
| `N` | Narrative | recounts events. Someone did or said something at some time. Includes speech reported inside a story. |
| `D` | Doctrine | explains how things are: the nature of God, self, world, karma, sin, salvation. Similes and parables used to explain. Moral reasoning that stops short of a direct command. |
| `W` | Worship | addresses or adores the divine: hymn, psalm, prayer, invocation, praise, doxology, confession, blessing formula. |

## The mistake to avoid

**Do not let `D` swallow everything.** Doctrine is the easiest code to reach for
and the easiest to over-apply. A passage is only `D` if it *explains* something.
If it tells you to act, it is `L`. If it reports an event, it is `N`. If it
praises, it is `W`. Ask what the passage is doing before asking what it is about.

**A passage that explains *and* instructs takes both.** "This Life within all
living things hides beyond harm; scorn thou to suffer, then, for that which
cannot suffer. Do thy part!" is `DL` — dropping the `L` because the passage is
mostly explanation is the single most common mistake on teaching texts. Scripture
argues in order to instruct; both halves count.

**`N` needs an event.** Narrative reports something that happened: an actor, and
a time at which they acted. A general statement about what a person does, or
what anyone who does X will find, is `D` however concrete it sounds. "Abandoning
desires which shake the mind, he finds full comfort" is `D`, not `N`.

## Worked examples

| Passage | Codes | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "In the name of Allah, the Beneficent, the Merciful." | `W` | An invocation. Not a list; not doctrine. |
| "Fight in the way of Allah, and know that Allah is Hearer, Knower." | `LD` | An imperative first, with a statement about God attached. |
| "And He taught Adam all the names, then showed them to the angels." | `N` | A reported event. The names are mentioned, not listed. |
| "Thee alone we worship; Thee alone we ask for help." | `W` | Address to God. Nothing is being explained. |
| "Thou shalt not eat of it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die." | `LT` | A prohibition and its consequence. |
| "The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want." | `W` | Praise by address. |
| "Whoso doeth good, it is for his own soul." | `D` | Explains how things work. No command, no named reward. |
| "Those who believe shall have gardens underneath which rivers flow." | `P` | A named good outcome. |
| "And Jesus went up into the mountain." | `N` | Event, nothing more. |

## How to decide

**Law and Threat together are common and important.** "Thou shalt not eat of
it: for in the day that thou eatest thereof thou shalt surely die" is `LT`.
Do not collapse it to one. The same goes for `LP` — a command with a reward
attached.

**Narrative that contains a command.** If a story reports God or a teacher
issuing an instruction that still binds the reader, use both `N` and `L`. If
the instruction is purely internal to the story ("the king told his servant to
fetch water"), use `N` alone.

**Doctrine versus Narrative.** "And Jesus went into the temple" is `N`.
"The kingdom of heaven is like unto a grain of mustard seed" is `D` — a simile
explaining how something is. A parable told as a story with a lesson is `ND`.

**Doctrine versus Law.** `D` describes, `L` directs. "Anger is the root of
suffering" is `D`. "Abandon anger" is `L`. Many passages are `DL`.

**Worship versus Doctrine.** "The LORD is my shepherd" is `W` — it addresses
and praises. "God is merciful and just" stated as a fact about God is `D`.
A passage that praises by describing is often `WD`.

**An imperative is always `L`.** If the passage tells the reader, or a
congregation, or a disciple, to do or not do something, `L` goes on it whatever
else is there — including when the instruction arrives at the end of a long
explanation, and including gentler forms: "let him", "one should", "seek",
"abandon", "be thou". This is the most commonly missed code.

**Threat needs a consequence, not just disapproval.** Calling someone wicked is
not `T`. Saying they will be cast into the fire is.

**Threat and Violence are not the same thing, and this is the distinction the
taxonomy most often gets wrong.** `T` is rhetorical force aimed at a hearer:
somebody is being warned what will happen to them. `V` is description: harm
being done, to anyone, including to the speaker.

| Passage | Codes | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "If ye will not hearken, I will send a fire upon your cities." | `TV` | Warns the hearer, and the thing warned of is violence. |
| "And they stoned Stephen, calling upon God." | `NV` | An event. Nobody is being warned; the victim is not the audience. |
| "They shall beat their swords into plowshares." | `P` | Violence named only to say it ends. |
| "Woe unto you, for ye shall be cast down." | `T` | A warning with no violence described. |
| "He was wounded for our transgressions." | `DV` | Harm described, and the speaker's side is the victim. Not a threat. |
| "Fight them until persecution is no more." | `LV` | A command to do violence. Instruction, not warning. |

Being the victim is not being threatened. A passage where the speaker or their
people suffer is `V`, never `T` on that account alone. A `T` needs somebody in
the passage's audience who is being told what is coming for them.

**Promise needs a good outcome, not just good news.** Announcing that a child is
born is `N`. Saying that those who believe shall have everlasting life is `P`.

## When to assign nothing

Leave the labels empty for a passage that carries none of these forces: a
fragmentary line, a transitional connective, an editorial heading, a speaker
attribution, a line of verse too short to stand on its own. An empty result is a
real answer and is better than forcing a code. Expect roughly one passage in ten
to come back empty.

## Do not

- Do not label by topic. A passage *about* the law is not `L` unless it commands.
- Do not use more than three codes, and reach three only when all three are
  plainly and separately present.
- Do not skip a passage. Every input index must appear in your output.
- Do not reorder, merge, split, or rewrite the passages.
